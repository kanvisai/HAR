Extend the arm
==============
id: extend_arm
side: the arm that opens or reaches

The reach often lasts close to a second. The window can run from about 0.40 s
to about 1.15 s, and overlapping windows of the same arm are merged.

In that window, either the elbow opens, or a straight arm reaches away from
the torso.

Opening:
  The arm is valid on most frames. A missing point is not used.
  The elbow opens by at least 35 degrees, and it keeps opening through the window.
  It starts bent (angle 125 degrees or less) and ends open (angle 130 degrees or more).
  From above, the elbow can stay under 130 degrees if the hand finishes far
  from the body: at least 0.55 torso lengths from the shoulder–hip line, and
  at least 0.30 farther out than it started, with the elbow already past 100 degrees.
  The wrist ends at least 0.10 torso lengths farther from the shoulder than it started.

Reaching with the arm already straight:
  The wrist moves out from the shoulder–hip line by at least 0.35 torso lengths.
  It starts near the body and ends clearly away from it.
  The elbow stays open. This is the reach toward a shelf or into a cart, seen
  from above, where the elbow angle barely changes.

Distances use the frame's width-to-height ratio.
It does not depend on another action.
