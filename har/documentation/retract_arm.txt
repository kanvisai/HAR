Retract the arm
===============
id: retract_arm
side: the arm that closes or comes back

The fold often lasts close to a second. The window can run from about 0.40 s
to about 1.15 s, and overlapping windows of the same arm are merged.

In that window, either the elbow closes, or the hand comes back to the body.

Closing:
  The arm is valid on most frames. A missing point is not used.
  The elbow closes by at least 35 degrees, and it keeps closing through the window.
  It starts open (angle 145 degrees or more) and ends bent (angle 120 degrees
  or less), but not collapsed (above 45 degrees).
  The wrist ends at least 0.10 torso lengths closer to the shoulder than it started.
  If the wrist travels out from the torso, it is a reach, not a retract.

Coming back:
  The wrist starts away from the shoulder–hip line and ends near the body,
  moving in by at least 0.30 torso lengths. A short gap where the arm is not
  seen does not cancel it.

Distances use the frame's width-to-height ratio.
It does not depend on another action.
