Typical gestures in categories 0, 1 and 5

These notes come from example clips in /home/ignacio/Escritorio/Company/videos.
The camera sits at the end of the aisle, looking along it from above.
Category 0 in that set is the beer aisle only. Categories 1 and 5 also include
jams, canned goods and pulses. This is what the body does in those examples.
It is not a detector yet.

Category labels
  0  A person walks along the aisle and does nothing more than walk, plus an
     occasional spontaneous gesture.
  1  The customer walks along the aisle, stops to look, and does not buy.
  5  The customer walks along the aisle without stopping to look or to buy,
     but is already carrying objects.

0. Walks through
  The clips are about 10 to 20 seconds. The person enters and leaves without
  stopping. The arms hang and swing with the step. The hands stay near the
  thigh. The torso follows the direction of travel and does not turn toward
  the shelf. Sometimes there is a short loose gesture: one hand goes up to
  the face or the head, or both hands rest on a cart handle while the person
  keeps walking. There is no stop, and no arm reaching out to take a product.

1. Stops to look, and does not buy
  These clips are longer, often 15 to 35 seconds, and they appear in several
  aisles. The person arrives walking, slows down, and stays beside the shelf.
  The torso turns toward the shelf and the head drops toward the products.
  The arms stay down, or one hand rests on the cart or the basket. Sometimes
  there is a small lean, but the reach-grab-return cycle is not there. Then
  the person turns back toward the aisle and walks on. If they have a cart or
  a basket, it stops with them.

5. Walks through while carrying something
  The travel is the same as category 0: the person keeps walking and does not
  stand in front of the shelf. The difference is in the arms. At least one
  arm is bent and holds a basket, a bag or a product, and that hand moves
  with the body instead of swinging. Sometimes both hands are together in
  front, on a basket, or pushing a cart, and the cart does not stop to look.
  There is no sustained turn toward the shelf and no reach to take a product.

What separates them in these examples
  Categories 0 and 5 do not stop. Category 1 does, and turns the torso toward
  the shelf.
  Category 0 has free arms. Category 5 has at least one arm occupied and
  steady relative to the body.
  Carrying a cart or a basket is not enough to tell them apart, because it
  appears in all three.
