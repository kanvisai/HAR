What the program does
=====================

From a skeleton that is already computed, it marks arm and torso gestures
consistent with concealing a product after it is already in the hand. With 8
points the object, the bag and the clothes are not visible: each notice is a
movement that fits that, so it can be checked on the video.

Input
-----
poses_full.npy with shape (frames, 8, 2). Coordinates from 0 to 1, the y axis
grows downward. poses.npy is not used: it is not aligned frame by frame with the video.

Points, in this order:
  0 left shoulder
  1 right shoulder
  2 left elbow
  3 right elbow
  4 left wrist
  5 right wrist
  6 left hip
  7 right hip

Side is the person's, not the image's. The unit of distance is the torso, from
the shoulder center to the hip center. Height is 0 at the hip and 1 at the
shoulder; above the shoulder it goes past 1.

The abdomen is a low point on the torso, closer to the hip than to the shoulder.
The chest is the midpoint. The torso axis is the segment from that arm's
shoulder to its hip.

An arm frame is invalid if the torso is bad, if the upper arm or the forearm
is too short, or if the elbow closes to 40 degrees or less. That is usually
the tracker, especially from behind, and not a gesture.

How detection works
-------------------
Each gesture is searched separately on those measurements. The detail of each
one is in the txt named with its id. The order and the only dependency between
gestures are in dependencies.txt.

At the end, actions_config.json is read. "enabled": false removes that id from
the JSON and from the window. "enabled": true puts it back. If the file is
missing, or an id is missing, that action stays on. Save and run again.

Right now cover_with_forearm, turn_torso and crouch are false. The rest are true.

Three gestures from the original list cannot be seen with this pose and are not emitted:
open the backpack, bag, or cart; put an arm into a bag and pull it out empty;
cover the product with another item. They are noted in the JSON, under
not_observable_with_this_pose.

Programs
--------
All three live in the HAR_en folder. If no path is given, they use clip 61.

detect_json.py
  Writes actions.json next to the npy and also temp_output/actions.json.
  -o changes the JSON next to the npy. The temp_output copy is still written,
  unless it is the same file.

visualize_ske.py
  Opens the enlarged-skeleton window and writes temp_output/skeleton.mp4.

visualize_sup.py
  Opens the mp4 window with the skeleton on top and writes into temp_output
  actions.json, skeleton.mp4 and overlay.mp4.
  --video can point at another mp4.

The temp_output names are fixed: each run replaces the previous file.

Window
------
Space pauses. Left arrow, or A, steps back one frame. Right arrow, or D,
steps forward one frame. Q, or Escape, closes.

The list on the right starts empty. While an action is still going, the frame
gets a red border, the arm involved is drawn in red, and "In progress" appears
at the top. When it ends, a row is added with the time range, the name and the
side. At the end the window stays on the last frame.

What is in the JSON
-------------------
poses, video, fps, n_frames, duration_s, keypoints, note,
not_observable_with_this_pose, disabled_actions, and the actions list.

Each action has id, name, side (left, right or both), frame_start, frame_end,
t_start, t_end, duration_s and detail.
The detail is the measurement that triggered the notice.
